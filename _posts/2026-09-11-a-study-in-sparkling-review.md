After an intense year as a single mom, the divorce has been finalized, which makes Fiona relieved to put the relationship behind her and finally receive child support. She does not want to relive that experience by getting into another relationship. Satoshi is the owner of a popular wine bar and bookstore and is studying for the Master Sommelier exam, the world's most difficult exam. Between maintaining the wine bar and studying, he has no time for a relationship, much less one with his favorite customer, who he’s absolutely in love with. They cannot ignore their mutual attraction, so they agree to keep things casual, without a label, to blow off some steam, but what if they want something more?

OK, put a finger down if you ever read a book synopsis on Netgalley, that was so good that you requested the advanced copy, knowing that it’s the second book in a series, and then read the first book before you began reading the second book.

I'm missing a finger because I did just that. Before I was approved for A Study in Sparkling, I read the first book, An Academic Affair, so that I knew what I was getting into. It was just as fun to read if you enjoy enemies-to-lovers romance between two English professors who marry so they can work at the university. Spoilers below if you haven’t read A Study in Sparkling yet.

Fiona is trying to rebuild her life after a lengthy divorce from her husband, who revealed the year before that he had been living a double life throughout their marriage and was going to leave Fiona and their kids. If you’ve read the first book, you know how difficult it was for her, as she spent the entire year supporting her family without child support.

Divorce is one of the most stressful events anyone could go through. When it happens to someone like Fiona, who tied her identity to her partner, it’s no wonder she feels insecure around Satoshi, who seems perfect in Fiona’s eyes. The RELIEF I felt when the divorce was finalized in the first few chapters could refill a prescription for a heavy dose of antipsychotics. I fully expected the divorce to carry the main conflict throughout the book, but instead I got to read about Fiona starting over with Satoshi.

Do they have any business dating each other? Fiona thinks not, because of their age difference and where they both are in life. She tells him that he’s better off with someone younger who won’t hold him back as she will. I understand where her concerns stem from, since she wants Satoshi to be more than friends with benefits but doesn’t want another terrible breakup. It doesn’t stop Satoshi from reminding her of his interest in her and going above and beyond to make her and her family happy. I especially enjoy the parts with him and Lex, Fiona’s nonbinary child, because Satoshi stocks his wine bar and bookstore with LGBTQ+ middle-grade and young adult books so Lex can enjoy spending time there. Being pansexual himself, Satoshi is a great mentor to Lex as they navigate their identity as a nonbinary person. Talk about being the dad who stepped up!

What really convinced me to read this book in the first place was finding out that Satoshi was training to become a Master Sommelier, which meant I was going to read about different types of wine, and let me tell you, Satoshi served with the best talk about wine, the culture surrounding it, and the fine dining experience he gives. It was one of the things that kept me reading because I love it when food and drink are described in detail in books. I will definitely think of this book every time I see a nice bottle of wine.

My only critique of the story is the pacing throughout. I understand that the divorce is fresh and taking the relationship slowly is wise, but I thought it was too repetitive to have Fiona tell Satoshi that she wants to take things slow. It almost made the story feel slow and drawn out. Satoshi is very devoted to her, and his biggest flaw is that he tries to accommodate everyone without asking others for help. At the same time, he balances studying for his exam, his job, and his new relationship. By the end of the book, Fiona and Satoshi finally become girlfriend and boyfriend, with Fiona following her heart and not worrying what others think and Satoshi leaning on others for help when he needs it. Hopefully, the pacing won’t be an issue in the next book, since these series are almost always trilogies, allowing the author to wrap up the story neatly. That being said, all books are great as standalones if you prefer that.

I can’t wait for her next book. I have a feeling Isamu, Satoshi’s brother and owner of a winery, will be one of the love interests, which I’m hopeful for because it would mean more wine to read about. If you enjoy a friends-to-lovers romance with an age gap and wine conversations that will make you feel like an amateur sommelier, then this book is for you.
