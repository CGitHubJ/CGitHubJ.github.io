Roxie loves reading romance novels, and Anne Matthews is her favorite author. While walking home after her training session with her self-defense instructor, Roxie makes an off-handed wish about living in the next Ann Mitchell’s novel. Thanks to the mysterious fedora-wearing stranger, Roxie’s wish comes true, and she finds herself saying yes to a date with someone who is too good to be true.

Until her date tries to kill her, that is. Instead of living out Anne Matthews’ usual romance, Roxie realizes her book is a CRIME THRILLER! (And I thought I had bad dates.)

As Roxie tries to escape from her killer date, she’s led to Grant, or at least the car he's in, as Roxie’s trying to steal it. Grant is an anxious creative writing professor with a penchant for thrillers. Despite Grant trying to avoid deadly plot points and Roxie running headfirst into them, they make a great team. Still, they have to find the author to finish the story before the story finishes them first. Because they can't possibly have Happily Ever After, right?

This action-packed novel is perfect for romance readers who will enjoy the tropes the author (or authors?) shamelessly puts Roxie and Grant through. Roxie and Grant spot the plot points a mile away while searching for the author to write Roxie’s wrongful wish. I personally enjoy reading Roxie’s thoughts as she follows the storyline. She may be a romance reader, but she’s a thrill seeker who’s enjoying every bit of action she goes through. I can tell that Roxie understands her author’s writing process and tries to take control over it.

One trope the author, yes, Kate Erberle, never allows is the Mary Sue/Gary Stu trope, where the protagonists are built for mystery-thriller novels. There’s actually a great explanation why Roxie is great at fighting, and it’s not just something Roxie learned once, but it's actually something she practices and enjoys. It’s ironic that Grant and Roxie enjoy reading but aren't built for their favorite genres.

For a debut, the author had a great way of spinning the romance/thriller genres on their head. This book is great for readers who enjoy reading about books and adventures mixed with humor. Readers may not feel as grounded in the novel when it comes to meta tropes, as the plot is more romance than suspense, as promised.
